THE PERSON ASKING — $persona_name

$persona_description

Write the question as this person would really ask it: their words, their
situation, their reason for asking. Not the source document's vocabulary, and
not a neutral restatement of a fact.

TYPICAL, NOT AN EXAM — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE FOR THE QUESTION.

The question must be one this person would bring to a helpdesk unprompted,
knowing nothing about the document you are reading. You have the source in
front of you and they do not, and that asymmetry is what makes a generated
question go wrong: the cheapest way to make a question hard is to build a
puzzle out of the page, and nobody ever asks a puzzle.

Never write:

- **References to the source.** "According to the guidance…", "as stated on
  the page…", "in the table above…". The person asking cannot see the table.
- **Enumeration scaffolding.** "List all four criteria", "which of the
  following", "state the two conditions under which…". People ask about a
  situation; how many things are in the answer is what they are hoping to find
  out, not something they already know to ask for.
- **Bundling.** One sentence demanding three unrelated facts, because that
  makes the question harder. Nobody asks three unrelated things at once.
- **Trivia.** A fact that is in the content and answerable but that this person
  has no reason to want. Findable is not the same as asked.

**Difficulty comes from the situation, not the construction.** Where the item
must draw on several paragraphs or two packets, that describes what the ANSWER
draws on. It never licenses a question that announces its own difficulty. One
real situation that happens to need several facts, asked by someone who does
not know how many facts it will take — that is the shape.

**Typical is not vague.** A real person leaves out their age, their sector,
their category; a question missing those has no single correct answer and
cannot be scored. Supply whatever detail the answer turns on, phrased the way
this person would supply it — "our candidate is 30 and joining the trading
desk", not "for a candidate aged 30 in the financial services sector".
Realism is in the framing and the situation, never in withholding what makes
the answer determinate.

**The role is not a source of fact.** Read the description above for intent,
vocabulary, and what this person typically asks. Anything in it that reads as
a fact about the subject — a figure, a deadline, a rule — is not evidence and
must not reach the answer. Every fact comes from the supplied content.

IF THIS CONTENT SUPPORTS NOTHING THIS PERSON WOULD ASK, SAY SO.

Do not force it. A question this person would never ask teaches nothing, and
stretching the answer beyond the supplied content to reach something they care
about puts a wrong reference answer into the eval set. Return exactly this
instead, and a different source will be sampled:
